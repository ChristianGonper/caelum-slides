Imagine a quiet night at an european nuclear power plant. Systems operating, critical services running, staff on duty. And suddenly, a series of enemy UAS (Unmanned aerial systems) begin to enter the perimeter: not one, but dozens, with trajectories that are hard to predict. Now bring that question closer to this room. Imagine if this happenned to an european space critical infrastructure, private or public, like Cebreros tracking station, Andøya Space Port or Redu. An incident there would not cut Europe off from space overnight, but disruption to critical ground infrastructure would matter.

La imagen es [Antena](./assets/images/Cebreros_station_pillars.jpg)

The response gap
Detecting a drone is not the same as stopping it safely.

Detection is only the first step.

Every other option has constraints: RF/GNSS, HPM, kinetic, interceptors.

Space infrastructure cannot simply stop operating.

### Notas
Once a drone has been detected and tracked, a site operator still faces the hardest question: what can we safely do about it?

There are already response options. But a tracking station is not an empty test range. It has antennas, communication links, sensitive equipment and people on site. A response that affects those systems, or creates debris near them, may be difficult to accept, even if it stops the drone.

Our application identifies this as a potential gap in a layered counter-UAS system: an additional response option for selected propeller-driven targets, to be evaluated under the safety and operational constraints of space ground infrastructure.

The challenge is not just to stop a drone. It is to protect the site without creating another risk.


## 5
La imagen es [dron montado](./assets/images/drone-mounting-clean.png)

Our solution
R.A.R.: an acoustic effector for propeller-driven UAS

External track, targeted response.

Directional acoustic excitation to the propellers.

Mission denial: loss of control margin or propeller damage.

Reusability.

### notas

So, how does our proposed solution work? R.A.R. is designed to fit into an existing counter-UAS chain, not replace it. An external system detects, identifies and tracks the aircraft. Our effector receives that target information, points its acoustic emitter and controls the emission.

The technical hypothesis concerns the propellers. If controlled acoustic excitation produces a significant dynamic response in a susceptible rotating blade, it will induce perturbations which will reduce the aircraft’s control margin; in some cases we will even be able to reach critical damage in the propeller. In practical terms, the UAS may no longer be able to maintain the trajectory needed to continue its mission.

Furthermore, our system will be completely reusable and low cost per neutralization compared to traditional kinetic interceptors.

Our first commitment is therefore a ground-based demonstrator with measured performance and limitations. We will also develop an airborne payload for a third-party carrier. In that configuration, the carrier’s approach to the target remains outside R.A.R.’s scope; our contribution is the acoustic payload, its integration, terminal acquisition and pointing.

### Notas
But, how do we intend to move from an experimental concept to a viable company?

First, on technical feasibility: the physical principle is supported by our analytical and simulation work, and it is also discussed in recent published research on acoustic resonance for drone interdiction.

Second, on the revenue model: the initial offer would be a paid evaluation or pilot. The next step could be the sale and integration of a ground system or an acoustic payload, followed by optional maintenance and support.


Regarding production, we would keep the core knowledge and system definition in-house: the design, software, calibration, acceptance data and configuration control. Initial manufacturing would be subcontracted.

Regarding intellectual property, results and know-how would be protected as confidential. During incubation, we would assess patentability.

Finally, the financing: ESA BIC incentive, accelerators: EUDIS, NATO DIANA and additional sources: private equity, private / public loans (NEOTEC, ENISA / SODICAL).
